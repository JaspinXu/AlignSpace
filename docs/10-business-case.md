# Business Case and Pilot

## Customer and wedge

Initial customer: Singapore interior-design SME with 3–20 client-facing designers. Initial workflow: living-room lead qualification and pre-concept alignment.

## Alignment hypothesis

A shared, evidence-backed brief can reduce repeated clarification and help both participants explain the same design intent. Measure review time, avoidable revisions and agreement against a baseline; do not represent these hypotheses as achieved outcomes.

## Adoption

1. Designer sends project link after lead qualification.
2. Homeowner completes guided brief asynchronously.
3. Designer reviews summary before detailed workshop.
4. Pair resolves decisions and approves.
5. Designer exports to current workflow.

No CRM integration in the first pilot.

## Six-week pilot

1–2 SMEs, 10–20 consenting projects, 45-minute training, weekly metric/safety review, no purchasing/contact/contractual output.

Success:

- ≥60% invitation-to-draft completion.
- Median designer review ≤10 minutes.
- ≥70% designers rate actionability ≥4/5.
- No P0 privacy/security incident.
- At least one workflow metric improves against baseline without hurting satisfaction.

## Defensibility

Not the general model: the two-party preference graph, evidence corrections, conflict taxonomy, question policy, evaluation set, and workflow integration. Learning data requires rights and privacy controls.

## Social impact

Reduces vocabulary barriers, makes rationale/limits transparent, supports future multilingual access, and retains human-assisted/manual paths.

## Commercial risks

Designers protect relationship work; homeowners avoid pre-work; liability perception; unexpected integration need. Mitigate with optional narrow pilot and measured impact.
