# User Research Plan

## Decisions to unlock

1. Which ambiguity causes the most expensive early rework?
2. Which living-room attributes make a brief minimally useful?
3. How many questions feel helpful rather than burdensome?
4. What evidence makes designers trust an AI proposal?
5. When should the system stop and hand off to humans?

## Sample

- 5–8 homeowners who renovated in the last two years or plan to within a year.
- 3–5 Singapore SME interior designers, including a studio lead.
- Vary renovation experience, budget, housing type, and digital comfort.

This is formative research, not a representative survey.

## Homeowner walkthrough (40 minutes)

1. Explain consent, recording, use, and withdrawal.
2. Ask for 3–5 references they would genuinely share.
3. Ask “What do you like here?” before probing.
4. Ask what they dislike or would not pay for.
5. Compare two similar references and ask what difference matters.
6. Ask how they would know the designer understood.
7. Show a draft brief and ask for corrections aloud.

Avoid leading with features or design labels.

## Designer contextual interview (45 minutes)

- Walk through first client conversation and artifacts.
- Identify questions that repeatedly surface later.
- Rank ambiguity cost across style, layout, function, material, lighting, budget, maintenance, timeline.
- Mark brief fields required/optional/unhelpful.
- Ask which provenance/confidence enables correction.
- Explore project volume, roles, handover, retention, and approval.

## Paired prototype test

1. Homeowner uploads references and completes interview.
2. Designer adds one deliberate feasibility constraint.
3. Pair resolves one seeded conflict.
4. Each privately completes the same 20-item interpretation rubric.
5. Pair approves or rejects the brief.

Capture time, questions, corrections, disagreements, confidence, and reactions.

## Prompts

### Homeowner

- Tell me about the last time you explained a visual preference.
- Which part was hardest to put into words?
- What should the designer copy, reinterpret, and ignore?
- What would make an AI interpretation unsafe or intrusive?
- Which decisions do you want the designer to make?

### Designer

- Show me a recent reference set that was hard to interpret.
- Which missing decision caused the largest revision?
- What makes a brief actionable enough to start concept work?
- Which recommendations require site/specialist verification?
- What would stop your team adopting this during intake?

## Data handling

Store participant code, role, notes, task metrics, and consent. Do not put names, contacts, private photos, recordings, or re-identification keys in the repository. Delete recordings on the agreed date.

## Synthesis

For each observation: evidence and participant code; context; behaviour; inferred need; confidence/counter-evidence; product implication; next validation. Promote to a finding only with repeated or strong workflow evidence; otherwise label a hypothesis.

## Exit criteria

- Designers agree on P0 fields or disagreements are documented.
- Top five ambiguity drivers are ranked.
- At least four of five late paired sessions finish without moderator rescue.
- Users distinguish AI-proposed, human-confirmed, and designer-constraint states.
- No unresolved critical privacy/trust issue remains for pilot.
